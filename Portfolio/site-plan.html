Overview

Purpose

The purpose of this website is to help myself, Kaitlyn Johnson, have an edge with applying for grad school applications. The website will provide writing samples, a personal statement, and contact information for Kaitlyn Johnson. This will help provide the author with an easy way to show diversity within the English domain and show some of the works Kaitlyn Johnson has completed during her undergraduate degree.

Audience

The main audience for this portfolio will be the graduate school admissions boards for Oxford and Cmabridge universities. In order to adapt to the strict demands of those high level academic groups, the website will have clearly formatted paragraphs, intentional vocabulary, and rhetorical structure for the website.

Dynamic elements

The two portions of the website that will have dynamic elements will be the contact form that will be provided on one of the pages and the page that contains blocks that contain the works Kaitlyn Johnson has created. That will ensure that the author can see how many people have interacted with the works she has posted.

Content

Home page

My name is Kaitlyn Johnson, and I am a senior at BYUI. My passion is to help other people be able to clearly communicate and feel confident in their writing styles. Over the course of this website, you will find information about who I am, what my credentials are, and how to get into contact with me. Thank you for spending the time to get to know me!

[Page 2]

I'm Kaitlyn Johnson, a writer, educator, and advocate for the transformative power of literature.

I grew up in a small Arizona town where higher education wasn't the norm—where only 17% of adults held college degrees and pursuing one was often discouraged. But a handful of community college professors saw something in me and fought alongside me for my education. That experience shaped everything that followed: a deep belief that everyone deserves access to learning, and that literature has the power to change lives.

My passion for making literature accessible took root at my local public library, where I noticed the classical fiction section gathering dust and youth programs dwindling. As a library assistant, I advocated for adding canonical works to our collection and took over programming. Within months, our children's Storytime grew from three attendees to over fifteen each week. I launched TEEN, STEM, and LEGO Clubs that are still running today. One of my proudest moments was watching a teenager check out a classical novel I'd recommended for purchase—proof that when we make great literature available, people reach for it.

I will graduate Magna Cum Laude with great distinction from Brigham Young University - Idaho with a degree in English and a minor in computer science. That cross-disciplinary path taught me that writing cleaner code makes you a better writer, and studying literature helps you think more clearly about technology. I've spoken on panels about AI's impact on education, tutored students learning English for the first time, and professionally edited manuscripts for authors and academics. I'm certified in Adobe InDesign and skilled in the full Adobe suite.

Currently, I'm am hoping to pursue my MSt in English (1700-1830) at Oxford or in English Studies from Cambridge, diving deep into Enlightenment and Romantic literature. My research focuses on writers like Rachel Speght and Mary Shelley—women who used their voices to challenge, inspire, and reshape their worlds.

My goal is simple: to become the kind of English professor who advocates for students the way mine advocated for me, to create spaces where everyone feels confident in their ability to communicate, and to keep great literature in rotation and accessible to all. I believe that every individual has their own way of communicating, their own story to tell, and their own contribution to make to the larger global conversation.
